Cinnamon French Toast

Thick slices of bread soaked in cinnamon-vanilla custard and pan-fried golden — a cosy weekend breakfast in 20 minutes.

⏱ 20 min🔥 ≈350 kcal per serving🍽 Serves 4📊 Easy🏷 Breakfast
4.9 · 37 ratings

Prep: 10 minutes  ·  Cook: 10 minutes  ·  Method: Pan-fried until golden

Why you will love this cinnamon French toast

This is the breakfast that makes a slow morning feel special — thick bread soaked in a cinnamon-vanilla custard, fried in butter until the edges go crisp and golden, then drowned in maple syrup. It tastes like a café breakfast, and it takes 20 minutes.

Cinnamon French Toast — full recipe

Servings:Serves 4

Ingredients

  • For the French toast
  • 8 slices thick bread (brioche or white)
  • 3 large eggs
  • ¾ cup milk (180 ml)
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • ¼ tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp butter (for frying)
  • To serve
  • Maple syrup
  • Fresh berries
  • Powdered sugar (optional)

Method — step by step

  1. Step 1 — Whisk the custard. Whisk the eggs, milk, sugar, cinnamon, vanilla and salt in a wide, shallow bowl until smooth.
  2. Step 2 — Dip the bread. Dip each slice into the custard for about 10 seconds per side — soaked, not soggy.
  3. Step 3 — Fry until golden. Melt the butter in a pan over medium heat and fry the slices for 2–3 minutes per side until golden brown.
  4. Step 4 — Keep warm and repeat. Keep the cooked slices warm in a low oven while you fry the rest.
  5. Step 5 — Serve with syrup. Stack the French toast, pour over warm maple syrup, and top with berries and a dusting of powdered sugar.

What to serve with it

This French toast is a breakfast on its own, but it is especially good with:

  • With bacon or sausages — salty and sweet together.
  • With scrambled eggs — a full weekend breakfast plate.
  • With coffee — hot coffee and warm maple syrup.
  • With whipped cream — a little extra for a treat morning.
  • On its own — syrup, berries and a fork.

Chef tips

  • Use day-old bread — it soaks up the custard without falling apart.
  • Do not soak the bread too long — 10 seconds per side is enough.
  • Keep the heat on medium — high heat burns the outside before the inside sets.
  • Butter the pan between batches — it keeps the toast golden, not greasy.
  • Warm the maple syrup — cold syrup cools the toast down.

Variations

  • Stuffed: spread cream cheese and jam between two slices before dipping.
  • Banana: top with sliced banana and a drizzle of honey.
  • Chocolate: add 1 tbsp cocoa powder to the custard.
  • Orange: add orange zest to the custard for a citrus lift.
  • Savoury: skip the sugar and cinnamon, add cheese and herbs instead.

Make ahead & storage

Best eaten fresh and hot. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 2 days — reheat them in a toaster or a hot pan to bring back the crisp edges. You can freeze cooked slices for up to 1 month and toast them straight from frozen.

Frequently asked questions

What bread is best?
Thick-sliced brioche, challah or plain white bread — soft bread falls apart, thin bread goes soggy.

Why is my French toast soggy in the middle?
The bread soaked too long, or the pan was not hot enough. Dip briefly and fry on medium heat.

Can I make it dairy-free?
Yes — use oat milk and fry in a little oil instead of butter.

Can I make the custard ahead?
Yes — whisk it the night before and keep it covered in the fridge.

Kitchen equipment you will need

  • A wide, shallow bowl
  • A whisk
  • A large frying pan
  • A spatula
  • A plate (for serving)

Use day-old bread. Slightly stale bread soaks up the custard without falling apart — fresh bread just goes soggy.
