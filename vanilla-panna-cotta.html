Vanilla Panna Cotta

Silky vanilla cream set into a delicate panna cotta, topped with a bright berry compote — an elegant dessert that takes 20 minutes to make and 4 hours to chill.

⏱ 20 min + 4 hrs chill🔥 ≈280 kcal per serving🍽 Serves 4📊 Easy🏷 Desserts
4.9 · 33 ratings

Prep: 20 minutes  ·  Cook: 5 minutes  ·  Method: Set in the fridge, no baking

Why you will love this vanilla panna cotta

This is the dessert that looks like it came from a restaurant but takes almost no effort — just warm cream, vanilla and gelatin, poured into glasses and left to set. The result is silky, wobbly and cool, with a bright berry compote on top that cuts through the richness.

Vanilla Panna Cotta — full recipe

Servings:Serves 4

Ingredients

  • For the panna cotta
  • 2 cups heavy cream (500 ml)
  • ½ cup whole milk (120 ml)
  • ⅓ cup sugar (65 g)
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 2 tsp powdered gelatin
  • 3 tbsp cold water
  • For the berry compote
  • 1½ cups mixed berries (200 g)
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • To serve
  • Fresh berries
  • Mint leaves

Method — step by step

  1. Step 1 — Heat the cream. Warm the cream, milk, sugar and vanilla in a saucepan over medium heat until the sugar dissolves — do not let it boil.
  2. Step 2 — Bloom the gelatin. Sprinkle the gelatin over the cold water and leave it for 5 minutes until spongy, then stir it into the warm cream until fully dissolved.
  3. Step 3 — Pour into glasses. Pour the cream mixture into 4 glasses or ramekins and leave to cool for 10 minutes.
  4. Step 4 — Chill until set. Cover and chill in the fridge for at least 4 hours, or overnight, until the panna cotta is set but still wobbly.
  5. Step 5 — Serve with compote. Warm the berries, sugar and lemon juice in a small pan for 3 minutes until saucy, cool slightly, and spoon over the panna cotta.

What to serve with it

This panna cotta is a light finish to a rich meal, but it is especially good with:

  • With fresh berries — the compote is already there, so add a few extra on the side.
  • With shortbread — a buttery biscuit next to the silky cream.
  • After a big dinner — light, cool and not too sweet.
  • At a dinner party — make it the day before and serve it straight from the fridge.
  • On its own — it needs nothing else.

Chef tips

  • Do not let the cream boil — it dulls the vanilla flavour and can make the gelatin set grainy.
  • Use room-temperature gelatin water — cold water blooms it evenly.
  • Strain the mixture through a sieve before pouring — it catches any undissolved gelatin.
  • Chill for at least 4 hours — a wobbly centre means it needs more time.
  • Serve it in the glass — no unmoulding stress, and it looks beautiful.

Variations

  • Chocolate: stir 2 tbsp cocoa powder into the warm cream.
  • Coffee: add 1 tbsp instant coffee to the cream for a mocha panna cotta.
  • Coconut: swap the milk for coconut milk and top with mango.
  • Lemon: add lemon zest to the cream and serve with lemon curd.
  • Strawberry: top with sliced strawberries and a drizzle of honey instead of compote.

Make ahead & storage

Perfect for making ahead — it keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days covered. Make the compote separately and spoon it on just before serving. Not suitable for freezing.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my panna cotta rubbery?
Too much gelatin — measure it carefully, and do not add extra "to be safe".

Can I use gelatin leaves instead of powder?
Yes — soak 3 leaves in cold water for 5 minutes, squeeze out the water, then stir them into the warm cream.

Can I make it dairy-free?
Yes — use full-fat coconut milk instead of cream and milk.

How do I know it is set?
Give the glass a gentle shake — it should wobble like jelly, not slosh like liquid.

Kitchen equipment you will need

  • A small saucepan
  • 4 glasses or ramekins
  • A whisk
  • A small bowl (for blooming the gelatin)
  • A fine sieve

Do not let the cream boil. Gentle heat keeps the vanilla bright and the gelatin smooth — that is the whole secret of a silky panna cotta.
